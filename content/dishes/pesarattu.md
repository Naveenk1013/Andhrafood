## Story
Pesarattu is the crown jewel of Coastal Andhra morning tiffins. Made from whole green gram (pesalu) ground fresh without fermentation, it boasts a golden-green crust, delicate earthiness, and crisp edges. A handful of raw rice gives the crepe its characteristic crunch, while fresh ginger and green chillies ground directly into the batter bring gentle warmth and aid digestion. Paired with sweet-and-spicy Allam Pachadi, it forms an unbeatable morning ritual.

## Method
1. Rinse 1 cup of whole green gram and 2 tablespoons of raw rice thoroughly. Soak in 3 cups of water for 4 to 6 hours. Drain, reserving a splash of soaking liquid.
2. Transfer soaked lentils to a wet grinder or blender jar along with sliced fresh ginger, green chillies, and cumin seeds. Grind to a slightly grainy, pouring-consistency batter using minimal water.
3. Stir in salt to taste. The batter needs no fermentation and is ready to spread immediately.
4. Heat a heavy cast-iron tawa over medium-high heat. Smear lightly with sesame oil or ghee.
5. Pour a generous ladleful of batter into the center of the tawa and spread outwards in concentric circles into a thin, even circle.
6. Drizzle half a teaspoon of oil around the circumference. Scatter finely chopped raw onions, green chillies, and ginger on top, pressing them gently with a spatula into the setting batter.
7. Cook until the underside turns deep golden-brown and the edges release cleanly from the pan. Fold into a half-moon and serve piping hot.

## Notes and variations
- **MLA Pesarattu:** A popular Vijayawada and legislative assembly canteen specialty where a scoop of warm, fluffy semolina upma is tucked inside the hot crepe before folding.
- **Grinding texture:** Keep the batter slightly coarse rather than silky-smooth for the best rustic bite.

## Cultural notes
Unlike urad-dal dosas that require overnight fermentation, Pesarattu batter is cooked fresh, making it light, protein-dense, and cooling according to regional Ayurvedic tradition.
