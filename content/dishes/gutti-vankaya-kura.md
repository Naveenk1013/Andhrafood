## Story
Gutti Vankaya Kura is the undisputed masterpiece of Andhra vegetarian banquets. Tender, small purple eggplants are slit into four petals while retaining their crown, stuffed generously with a roasted spice paste of groundnuts, sesame seeds, coriander, cumin, dry coconut, and fiery Guntur red chillies. Slow-simmered in a thick tamarind-onion gravy until the eggplants turn melt-in-the-mouth soft and oil glazes the rim, this curry embodies the harmonious union of nutty richness, tongue-tingling spice, and deep sourness.

## Method
1. Dry-roast roasted groundnuts, white sesame seeds, coriander seeds, cumin seeds, dry coconut flakes, and whole Guntur red chillies on low heat until fragrant. Cool and grind to a coarse aromatic powder.
2. Mix half of the ground spice blend with grated onions, ginger-garlic paste, turmeric, salt, and half the tamarind pulp to form a thick stuffing paste.
3. Wash baby eggplants and trim the stem slightly, leaving the stalk firmly attached. Make a deep 'X' slit from the bottom up to three-quarters toward the stalk. Immerse immediately in salted water to prevent discoloration.
4. Stuff each eggplant cavity generously with the spice paste, wiping any excess off the skin.
5. Heat peanut oil in a wide, heavy-bottomed pan or earthen pot. Add mustard seeds, cumin seeds, and a sprig of fresh curry leaves for the popu.
6. Gently arrange the stuffed eggplants in a single layer in the hot oil. Fry on medium-low flame for 6 to 8 minutes, gently turning them with a spoon until the skin softens and blisters lightly.
7. Pour in the remaining spice powder, the rest of the tamarind extract, and 1 cup of warm water. Cover with a tight-fitting lid and simmer on low heat for 15 to 20 minutes until the eggplants are completely tender and the gravy thickens to a glossy finish.

## Notes and variations
- **Rayalaseema style:** Often adds more roasted groundnuts and garlic cloves into the stuffing for an earthier, thicker gravy.
- **Coastal Andhra style:** Emphasizes fresh grated coconut and a hint of jaggery (bellam) to balance the intense tamarind acidity.

## Cultural notes
No Andhra wedding feast or special celebration is considered complete without Gutti Vankaya Kura served alongside hot rice, fragrant ghee, and crisp appadalu.
